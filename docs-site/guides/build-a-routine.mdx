---
title: "Build a routine"
description: "From a one-off chat to a scheduled automation that runs itself."
---

The best routines start life as a chat you got working, then get scheduled. This
guide walks the full flow with a concrete example: a **morning ops briefing**.

## 1. Prototype it in chat

Open a new **Chat** and describe the whole task, including where the data comes
from and where the result should go:

```text
Every morning: pull yesterday's signups and failed payments from the app
database, summarize the top themes in 5 bullets, and draft a short note I can
post to #ops. Keep it under 150 words.
```

Let it run. Attach any files it needs, connect Slack and the database if you
haven't. Iterate until the output is exactly what you'd want to receive at 8am.

<Tip>
  Be specific about format and length. "5 bullets, under 150 words" produces a
  far more useful recurring artifact than "summarize it".
</Tip>

## 2. Save it as a routine

Click **Save as routine**. The builder opens prefilled with:

- the **instruction** from your chat,
- the **connectors**, **skills**, and **data sources** you used,
- and the agent's **model** choice.

Give it a clear name like `Morning Ops Briefing`.

## 3. Schedule and test

Pick when it runs:

- a preset such as **every weekday at 8am**, or
- a **cron** expression for full control.

Then choose **Create & test run** to fire it once immediately and confirm the
output before it goes live.

## 4. Add an alert

Add an **alert rule** so the result reaches you where you want it, and so you hear
about failures. For a briefing you might alert on **every run**; for a silent
guardrail routine you might alert **only on failure**.

## 5. Iterate any time

A routine is still an agent. Open it and **chat with it** to adjust the
instruction or debug a bad run, no need to wait for the next scheduled fire.

<Check>
  You now have an agent that does real work on a schedule and tells you about it,
  built by demonstrating the task once, not by configuring it blind.
</Check>
